Adding a new room, renovating a kitchen, or building a boundary wall are massive investments. Unfortunately, the building industry is famous for "bakkie builders" who talk a good game, take a massive upfront deposit, and then disappear—leaving you with half-built walls and empty pockets.

Protecting yourself starts before the first brick is laid. If you are hiring a builder in the East Rand, here are the five non-negotiable questions you need to ask during the quoting phase.

### 1. "Can I speak to your last three local clients?"
Pictures of beautiful houses on a WhatsApp profile are easy to fake or steal from the internet. Real reputations aren't. A reputable builder will proudly hand over references. Call them and ask the hard questions: *Did they finish on time? Did they stay on budget? Did they clean up the site?* ### 2. "Who will actually be on-site managing the project?"
Many contractors win your business with their charm, but then hand the actual work over to unsupervised, day-labor subcontractors while they go hunt for more jobs. You need to know who the dedicated site manager is going to be and whether they will be present every single day to ensure quality control.

### 3. "How do you handle scope changes and unexpected costs?"
Renovations are unpredictable. You might open a wall and find rotten plumbing. Ask the builder upfront how these surprises are billed. A professional contractor will have a formal "Change Order" process, meaning they will quote you for the extra work and require your signature *before* they proceed. 

### 4. "What is the payment schedule?"
**Never pay 100% upfront.** Never even pay 75% upfront. A standard, fair payment schedule is tied to project milestones. For example:
* 30% deposit to secure materials and start the site prep.
* 30% when the roof/walls go up.
* 30% when the plumbing and electrical are roughed in.
* 10% "retention" fee paid *only* after the final snag list is complete and you are fully satisfied.

### 5. "Are you registered with the NHBRC?"
The National Home Builders Registration Council (NHBRC) is there to protect consumers. If a builder is building a new home structure from scratch, they are legally required to be registered. Even for smaller renovations, NHBRC registration is a massive green flag that shows they are a legitimate, accountable business.
