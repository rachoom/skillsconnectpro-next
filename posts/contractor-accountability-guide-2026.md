If you switch on the news right now, you are likely to hear about the Madlanga Commission. The entire country is gripped by conversations around accountability, missing funds, and the devastating cost of hiring the wrong people for critical jobs.

While we watch transparency unfold on a national scale, it begs a very practical question for everyday South Africans: **Who is ensuring accountability in your own home?**

The home repair and renovation industry has long been plagued by its own version of "dodgy operators." We have all heard the horror stories of a "bakkie builder" who quoted a bargain price, demanded a 70% upfront deposit for materials, and then vanished into thin air, leaving behind a half-finished roof and a blocked number.

At **Skills Connect Pro**, we believe that transparency shouldn't just be for politicians. Here are the three red flags that signal a contractor is dodging accountability—and how to protect your hard-earned money.

### 1. The "Paperless" Promise
If a contractor scribbles a quote on the back of a cigarette box or only gives you a price verbally over WhatsApp, walk away. Dodgy operators hate paper trails. A professional artisan—whether they are a plumber, an electrician, or a steel fabricator—will provide a formal, itemized quote. You need to know exactly what you are paying for regarding labor versus materials. 

### 2. The Urgent Deposit Trap
Materials cost money, and it is standard practice for a builder to request a deposit to secure them. However, if a contractor demands an exorbitant upfront fee (like 80% of the total project cost) and insists it must be paid *today* in cash to "secure the special price," it is a massive red flag. Professional artisans work on staggered milestone payments tied to actual progress on your property.

### 3. The Ghost Reputation
In today’s digital age, a complete lack of a track record is suspicious. If a contractor cannot provide references, has no portfolio of past work, and operates strictly through a prepaid cell phone number, they are essentially a ghost. When things go wrong, ghosts disappear.
