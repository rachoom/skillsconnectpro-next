# Cracks in Your Walls: Is It Normal Settling or a Structural Hazard?

Every house moves. Changes in temperature, shifting soil, and moisture levels cause properties to expand and contract slightly over time. Because of this, finding a small crack in your plaster is highly common.

However, how do you know when a crack is just cosmetic or a warning sign that your house is experiencing foundation failure? Knowing what to look for can save your property from catastrophic damage.

## The Warning Signs of Serious Structural Cracks

While minor hairline cracks are usually nothing to worry about, you should immediately pay attention if you notice the following patterns:

*   **Large Width:** Any crack that is wider than 5mm (roughly the width of a wedding ring or a pencil) warrants a professional inspection.
*   **Horizontal or Diagonal Stair-Step Cracks:** Cracks that run horizontally along mortar joints or form a distinct "stair-step" pattern in brickwork are strong indicators of foundational shifting or structural movement.
*   **Cracks Above Door and Window Frames:** If a crack starts at the corner of a door frame or window and shoots diagonally upward toward the ceiling, the wall may be sagging or losing support.
*   **Doors and Windows Jamming:** If cracks are accompanied by doors or windows that suddenly stick, jam, or refuse to close properly, the structural frame of the house is likely warping.

## What Causes Major Cracks?
Severe cracks are often caused by poor initial construction foundation work, soil erosion from broken underground pipes, or mature tree roots lifting the base of the walls.

## Don't Just Paint Over It
Simply filling a major structural crack with plaster and painting over it will not solve the problem; the crack will inevitably reappear as the building continues to move. If you notice any of the warning signs listed above, it is vital to get a professional builder or structural expert to assess the root cause.
