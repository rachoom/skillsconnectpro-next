# Stubborn Blocked Drains? When Plunging Isn't Enough

We all deal with the occasional slow-draining shower or clogged kitchen sink. Usually, a few vigorous pumps with a plunger or cleaning out the hair trap gets things flowing again. 

But what happens when the plunger doesn't work? If water is backing up into your shower when you flush the toilet, or if you constantly smell a foul, sewage-like odor around your property, you are dealing with a deep system blockage. 

## What Causes Deep System Blockages?

When multiple drains in your house are slow or backing up simultaneously, the problem lies deep within your main sewer line. Common causes include:

*   **Tree Root Intrusion:** Tree roots are naturally drawn to the moisture and nutrients inside sewer pipes. They can easily crack older clay or PVC pipes, growing inside and creating an impenetrable net that catches all waste.
*   **The FOG Blockage (Fats, Oils, and Grease):** Pouring cooking oil down the kitchen sink is a disaster waiting to happen. As the grease cools in the underground pipes, it solidifies into massive, rock-hard blockages.
*   **Collapsed Pipes:** Older pipes can simply succumb to the pressure of shifting soil or heavy vehicles parking overhead, collapsing entirely and blocking the flow of waste.

## Why Chemical Cleaners Are a Bad Idea

When faced with a stubborn blockage, many people reach for heavy-duty chemical drain cleaners. This is often a mistake. 
1.  **They rarely work on deep clogs:** Chemicals often cannot reach or dissolve massive root intrusions or solid grease blocks.
2.  **They damage your plumbing:** The intense heat generated by chemical reactions can melt PVC pipes or corrode older metal plumbing, turning a simple blockage into an expensive pipe replacement.

## The Professional Solution

Severe blockages require heavy-duty equipment like high-pressure hydro-jetters to blast away roots and grease, or specialized drain cameras to inspect the structural integrity of the pipe underground.
