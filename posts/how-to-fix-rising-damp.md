# How to Identify and Treat Rising Damp Before It Destroys Your Walls

Few home maintenance issues are as stubborn and destructive as rising damp. It starts silently beneath your floorboards or behind your skirting boards, and by the time you notice the peeling paint or smell the musty odor, it has already taken hold of your brickwork.

Ignoring damp doesn't just ruin your home's aesthetics; it also degrades the structural integrity of your walls and creates a breeding ground for mold that can trigger respiratory issues.

## What is Rising Damp?
Rising damp occurs when groundwater travels upward through the porous bricks and mortar of your walls via capillary action—much like liquid soaking upward through a piece of paper towel. 

In normal construction, a plastic barrier called a **Damp Proof Course (DPC)** is installed to block this moisture. If your home's DPC is old, damaged, or was never installed correctly, rising damp is the inevitable result.

## Common Signs of Rising Damp

*   **Bubbling or Peeling Paint:** Paint on the lower section of your walls (usually up to 1 meter high) begins to blister, flake, or peel away.
*   **Efflorescence (White Powdery Salt):** As the water evaporates out of the brickwork, it leaves behind a white, powdery residue of ground salts on the surface of the wall.
*   **Decaying Skirting Boards:** Wooden skirting boards that become soft, warped, or show signs of fungus/rot.
*   **Damp Patches:** Dark tide marks or physically damp spots on internal plasterwork that feel cold to the touch.

## How to Fix It Permanently
Many homeowners make the mistake of scraping off the bubbling paint, applying a standard primer, and repainting. Within a few months, the damp returns. To fix rising damp permanently, you must stop the moisture at the source.

1.  **Injecting a New DPC:** Professional damp-proofing artisans often drill holes into the mortar line and inject a specialized water-repellent cream or liquid that creates a new, chemical damp proof barrier.
2.  **Removing Contaminated Plaster:** Plaster that has been contaminated by groundwater salts must be hacked off completely and replaced with a specialized salt-retardant plaster mix.
3.  **Ensuring Correct Exterior Ground Levels:** Sometimes, external garden beds or paving are built up higher than the original DPC, allowing water to bypass the barrier entirely.
