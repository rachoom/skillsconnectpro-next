South Africans are naturally resourceful. When something breaks, our first instinct is often to grab a screwdriver, watch a quick YouTube tutorial, and try to fix it ourselves to save a few Rands. 

While that DIY spirit is great for fixing a leaky tap or painting a bedroom, applying it to your home's electrical grid is a recipe for disaster. 

Electrical work is highly regulated for a reason. A botched job doesn't just mean your lights won't turn on—it can lead to house fires, fried appliances, or fatal shocks. 

At **Skills Connect Pro**, we want you to stay safe. Here are five electrical faults you should never attempt to fix yourself, and exactly when it is time to call in a verified East Rand electrician.

### 1. The Main DB Board Keeps Tripping
If a specific switch on your Distribution Board (DB) trips once, it might just be an overloaded plug point. Unplug your heater or kettle and try again. 

However, if the main switch trips repeatedly, or if a switch refuses to stay up no matter what is plugged in, **stop immediately.** This indicates a dead short, a severe grounding issue, or a failing breaker. Opening the DB board yourself exposes you to live, un-fused municipal power. 

### 2. Sparks or Burning Smells from Outlets
If you plug something in and hear a popping sound, see a blue spark, or smell a faint "fishy" or burning plastic odor, the wiring behind the wall is melting. 
* **Do not** use that plug point.
* **Do not** try to unscrew the faceplate to look behind it.
Turn off the power to that room at the DB board and call a professional. 

### 3. Flickering Lights (When it isn't Loadshedding)
If your lights are flickering, dimming randomly, or surging in brightness, it is rarely just a bad lightbulb. This is usually a sign of a loose neutral wire or a deteriorating connection in your ceiling space. Loose connections cause electrical arcing, which generates intense heat and is the leading cause of electrical fires in homes.

### 4. Exposed or Illegal Wiring
If you've just moved into a new place—or if you are looking at wiring done by a previous "handyman" that looks like a rat's nest of electrical tape—do not try to tidy it up yourself. You have no idea which wires are live, and illegal connections often bypass the safety switches on your DB board entirely.

### 5. Upgrading or Adding New Circuits
Want to install a new high-power air conditioner, a heavy-duty oven, or a backup generator/inverter system? These appliances draw massive amounts of current. Simply wiring them into an existing plug point will overload the circuit. A certified electrician needs to calculate the load, run the correct gauge of wire, and install a dedicated breaker.

### The Bottom Line: Don't Risk It
A certified electrician has the testing equipment to find invisible faults safely and the training to ensure your home meets local compliance standards (which is required for your home insurance to remain valid).
