# No Hot Water? 4 Reasons Your Geyser Isn't Heating Up

Few things disrupt a morning routine faster than turning on the shower only to be met with an icy blast of water. If your geyser has stopped heating, you aren't alone—this is one of the most frequently searched plumbing emergencies for homeowners. 

Before you panic, here are the four most common culprits behind a cold geyser and what needs to be done to fix them.

## 1. A Faulty Element
The heating element is the component inside the geyser that actually warms the water. Over time, constant exposure to water and mineral buildup causes the element to corrode and eventually burn out. When this happens, the geyser will stop heating entirely. 

## 2. A Broken Thermostat
The thermostat acts as the brain of your geyser, monitoring the water temperature and telling the element when to turn on or off. If the thermostat fails, it can cut power to the element completely or, conversely, cause the water to dangerously overheat. 

## 3. The Element Trip Mechanism
Modern geysers have a safety trip switch built into the thermostat to prevent overheating. If there is a power surge or a minor electrical fault, this safety switch might pop. While it can sometimes be reset, a repeatedly tripping safety switch indicates an underlying hardware issue.

## 4. Power Supply or DB Board Issues
Sometimes the issue isn't the geyser itself, but the power getting to it. Check your main distribution (DB) board to see if the geyser circuit breaker has tripped. If it trips immediately after being flipped back up, do not force it—leave it off.

## What Should You Do?
Working with a geyser involves a dangerous combination of high-voltage electricity and pressurized hot water. Trying to replace a thermostat or element yourself without proper training can void your appliance warranty, cause severe injury, or lead to a burst tank.
